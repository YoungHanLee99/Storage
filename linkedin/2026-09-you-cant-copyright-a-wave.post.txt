You can't copyright a wave.

Our best-known work is one. Nobody owns waves, flowers, forests or thunderstorms. Yet ARTE MUSEUM has opened from Jeju to Dubai and New York, and this November, Nagoya. So what exactly do we own?

I get that question in almost every new city, from landlords, licensees and investors. My working answer, in five parts:

1. Copyright protects the how, not the what. Register everything, and register in layers.

2. The "feel" of a venue belongs to trademark and unfair competition law. File before you announce. Courts in the US, Japan and Korea have all protected the accumulated result of investment once it became recognizable.

3. Contracts are where IP is actually made. Chain of title with every contributor, and three clauses in every overseas partnership: ownership of derivatives, quality control, and the end.

4. The production pipeline is a trade secret, but only if it is managed like one.

5. AI: AI-assisted is registrable, AI-generated is not. The difference is documented human judgment.

IP is not a shield. It is infrastructure.
